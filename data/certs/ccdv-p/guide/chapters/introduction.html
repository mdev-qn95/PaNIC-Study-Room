About the exam

The Claude Certified Developer – Professional (CCDV-P) exam tests whether you can operate Claude applications, agents, and coding-agent fleets in production: instrument them, diagnose failures, gate releases with evaluations, control cost and latency, and harden tool-using agents against adversarial input. It assumes the scope of the Foundations certification.

Item Value (Exam Guide v1.0, effective November 2026)
Items 70 (60 scored, 10 unscored and unidentified)
Format Scenario-based multiple-choice and multiple-response; each item states how many responses to select
Time 120 minutes, proctored online or at a test center
Passing score Scaled 720 on a 100–1,000 scale
Validity 12 months; renewal is a free non-proctored assessment if done on time
Score report Pass/fail, scaled score, and percent-correct by domain

The source is the official exam guide, §4–5. The guide also names the products a candidate is expected to use daily: the Messages API, the Agent SDK, Claude Managed Agents, Claude Code, and MCP, plus the differences between first-party API and cloud-platform deployments.

How the exam items work

Every item is a production scenario with several plausible fixes. The three official sample items share a pattern: the correct answer addresses the cause the evidence points to, using a control that actually enforces the outcome. Retrying harder, switching to a bigger model, adding a stronger prompt, or only logging are the typical distractors. Read each chapter's Exam lens box with that pattern in mind. Exam-lens boxes are editorial inference, not official exam content.

How to read this guide

  • Each chapter maps to one of the 25 skills in the official blueprint. The official objective text is in the collapsible box under each chapter title.
  • A bracketed number such as [3] is a citation. Tap it to see the exact wording from the upstream source, a link to the page, and the date it was checked. All sources for a chapter are listed at its end.
  • Citation tags: Documented is behavior stated in official docs. Recommended practice is official guidance. Version-sensitive marks limits, betas, prices, and model-specific behavior that change often. Standard is a specification such as OpenTelemetry, MCP, OWASP, or NIST.
  • Sentences without a citation are engineering reasoning or recommended practice.
  • Chapter status: Fact-checked chapters passed an independent claim-by-claim review against the cited sources. Draft chapters have verified sources but no independent review yet.

Weights decide where your study time goes: Domain 2 (20%) and Domain 1 (17%) together are more than a third of the scored items.
